CURRENT RESULTS (NYGC 30x reference; rebuilt 2026-10-04)

Everything on this page now refers to the NYGC 30x high-coverage reference. The August 2026 matrix (old 1000G file), the “published FST values” table (never sourced) and the old sample sizes are withdrawn; the interactive heatmap and MDS below are drawn from the new matrix.

1. Overview

FST between each of the ten pairs of five populations: UZB (Uzbek, n = 1,256), EUR (633), SAS (601), EAS (585) and AFR (893), computed with PLINK 1.9 (Weir & Cockerham, --fst --within) on 82,744 LD-pruned SNPs shared between the Uzbek cohort and the NYGC 30x GRCh38 reference (AMR not used). The per-SNP analysis, top loci and the genome-wide distribution are on step 9; this page presents the matrix, its geometry and a check against unascertained SNPs.

Key finding. The Uzbek cohort is about equally close to South Asians (0.0143) and Europeans (0.0152); the distance to East Asians (0.0400) is 46% of EUR–EAS (0.0872) and 70% of SAS–EAS (0.0570), so Uzbeks lie between the Western Eurasian groups and East Asians. The distance to Africans (0.1310) is almost the same as SAS–AFR (0.1294). This agrees with ADMIXTURE K=4 (about 50% EUR-like, 30% EAS-like, 19% SAS-like; step 11) and with a non-negative fit of Uzbek allele frequencies as a mixture of the references (EUR 49%, EAS 28%, SAS 23%; step 9).

4. Classical MDS from FST Distance Matrix

Classical (metric) multidimensional scaling projects the 5 × 5 distance matrix into two dimensions (eigendecomposition of the double-centred squared distances; FST itself is used as the distance). The first axis carries 81.8% and the second 17.2% of the positive eigenvalues; the first separates AFR from everyone else, the second EUR from EAS, and UZB sits near SAS, slightly toward EAS relative to EUR. Because two dimensions cannot hold a 5-point distance matrix exactly, small distances such as UZB–SAS–EUR are only approximate in the plot; the table in section 3 is the exact result.

6. Interpretation

6.1. Where the Uzbek cohort sits

  • UZB–SAS (0.0143) and UZB–EUR (0.0152) are nearly equal. The difference (0.0010) is measurable but small (step 9); which of the two is “closer” depends on the reference panel, which contains no Central Asian groups.
  • UZB–EAS (0.0400) is less than half of EUR–EAS (0.0872) and smaller than SAS–EAS (0.0570). That is the FST expression of an East-Asian-like share of ancestry that Europeans and South Asians lack.
  • UZB–AFR (0.1310) equals SAS–AFR (0.1294) and is below EUR–AFR (0.1414), as expected when there is no African ancestry component (ADMIXTURE K=4: about 0%).

Historical explanations for these patterns (Indo-Iranian, Steppe, Turkic or Mongol contributions) are plausible context but were not tested here: the panel has no ancient or Central Asian reference samples.

6.2. Continental structure

  • AFR is farthest from every other group (0.129–0.167), EAS is farther from SAS (0.057) and EUR (0.087) than UZB is from either.
  • EUR–SAS (0.0314) is the closest pair among the three reference Eurasian groups, and both are close to UZB.

6.3. Check against unascertained SNPs (replaces the earlier “published values” table)

The earlier version of this page compared reference-pair values with “published 1000 Genomes FST ranges”. Those ranges have no source in the project records and are removed. In their place, FST was recomputed on chromosome 22 using all common biallelic SNVs (MAF ≥ 0.01; about 190,000 in the NYGC data, 60,979 in the imputed Uzbek data, 53,765 shared) instead of the LD-pruned array-based panel. Reference pairs: PLINK Weir & Cockerham; Hudson (Bhatia et al. 2013) estimator computed from allele frequencies for all pairs, used for the Uzbek pairs because the Uzbek and NYGC data are not in one PLINK file. The Hudson estimator reproduces PLINK on the reference pairs to within 0.005.

Two conclusions. (1) The ordering is the same in the panel and in the unascertained chr22 set: UZB is about equally close to SAS and EUR, closer to EAS than SAS and EUR are, and equal to SAS in distance from AFR. (2) The magnitudes are not the same. The array-based, LD-pruned panel gives smaller FST between Eurasian groups than all common SNVs (for example EUR–EAS 0.087 vs 0.104) and slightly larger FST to AFR for EUR (0.141 vs 0.126). This is SNP ascertainment: array SNPs are chosen to be common in the populations the array was designed for. Absolute FST values from this panel should therefore not be compared with published genome-wide values without that caveat. Chromosome 22 is a single chromosome, so these numbers carry sampling noise of the order of a few thousandths.

7. Methods

FST estimation: PLINK v1.9 --fst --within (Weir & Cockerham 1984) on the merged cohort + NYGC panel (merged_refqc, 82,744 SNPs, GRCh38). “Weighted” is the ratio-of-averages estimator.
MDS: classical metric MDS (double-centring of the squared distance matrix, two leading eigenvectors), implemented in JavaScript on this page and re-computed in Python for the static figure; both use the FST values directly as distances.
